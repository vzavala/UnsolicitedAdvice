# Not Every Paper Has to Be a Home Run

> *Unsolicited advice for young scientists.* Posted on LinkedIn on [April 29, 2025](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7322982279566233602/).

Sometimes we have the impression that *all* our papers need to be "home runs" (be super impactful). I feel that this often puts a lot of pressure on us. 

I think it is important to realize that papers are also often part of a bigger story that is contained in a collection/sequence of papers. As a result, a paper in isolation might not be particularly impactful, but the collection of papers might be. I like the analogy to baseball... frequent hits might be more effective than an isolated home run.

I think it is also important to realize that papers have different goals. For instance, even if one of your papers does not turn out to be particularly impactful, this might have been a vehicle for you to learn and acquire a new skill that will be useful for subsequent papers. 

Also, the impact of a paper is not always properly defined; if your work ends up being used by industry, you might not see this in citation counts (but it is clearly creating impact). In addition, papers are powerful mechanisms to help students gain knowledge, skills, and ultimately jobs.
