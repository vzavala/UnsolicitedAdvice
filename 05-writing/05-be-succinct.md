# Be Succinct

> *Unsolicited advice for young scientists.* Posted on LinkedIn on [March 23, 2025](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7309697567372546048/).

It is exponentially more difficult to write a 2-page proposal/paper/report than a 15-page one. This is because you really need to be *succinct* about what you want to communicate and how you want to communicate it. Being succinct is a skill that does not come easy and I initially found (and still find) super challenging. 

Some habits that I have developed over time are the following.

When writing a document I try to be as *word-efficient* as possible (regardless of length). For example, using the same word multiple times in a sentence or repeating the same idea many times in a document is not efficient, as it introduces redundancy and gets the reader tired/annoyed. Yes, you need some degree of repetition to reinforce key messages, but I am very selective/strategic about what I repeat. I also try to avoid long and complex sentences; if the idea is complex, I break it in multiple sentences.

I try to read the document *super carefully* to spot patterns in the words/sentences that allow me to condense ideas and to connect ideas across the entire document. I try to "zoom in" and "zoom out" from the document, so that I can identify redundancies in a paragraph and across paragraphs and to see how ideas connect in a paragraph and across paragraphs. I find this "multi-scale" view of the document to be effective at spotting redundancies and disconnected ideas. 

Perhaps it is the optimizer in me, but I take it as a personal challenge to minimize words as much as possible when writing. Ultimately, I try to remind myself that I want my document to be easy and enjoyable to read.
