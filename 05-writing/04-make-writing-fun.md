# Make Writing Fun

> *Unsolicited advice for young scientists.* Posted on LinkedIn on [July 31, 2023](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7091781145620578304/).

Many scientists struggle with technical writing; this is not an easy skill to acquire and requires lots of patience/practice. Sometimes it is challenging to get yourself motivated to write, as this can require significant intellectual energy and focus. 

One strategy that I use to motivate myself is to remind myself that writing is ultimately an art; as such, there are many ways in which I can connect my personality/interests to it or use it as a way to express myself. 

For instance, sometimes I focus on the storytelling aspects (e.g., narrative), sometimes I focus on tutorial aspects (e.g., how can I explain a complex concept), sometimes I focus on the technical aspects (e.g., structure and language), sometimes I focus on the aesthetics (e.g., fonts, visuals, text arrangement), sometimes I focus on the impact (e.g., who will read this? how to make it more "catchy"?), sometimes I use it as an idea generation tool, sometimes I use it as an anxiety mitigation tool (e.g., your thoughts become more controlled when you write), and sometimes I just nerd out (e.g., think about new words that I can use, how to hide fun messages in the text, or think about patterns in the text). 

Ultimately, I like to think of writing as a game; you need to keep it varied and make it fun so that it can be enjoyable/sustainable. Every time I write something I try to find a new angle or something new that this is teaching me. Sometimes it is extremely difficult to get started/excited; but, in my experience, once you break the initial mental barrier, the ideas start flowing and it can be quite fun. In this respect it feels a lot like exercising; you feel a mental block at first but once you start you cannot stop. 

Yes, there are some best/standard practices that you should follow with writing, but ultimately you should try to make it fun for *you*.
