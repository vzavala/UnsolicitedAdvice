# Writing Proposals for Multidisciplinary Panels

> *Unsolicited advice for young scientists.* Posted on LinkedIn on [January 17, 2024](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7153182763238522880/).

Sometimes your proposals will be reviewed by a multi-disciplinary panel that is composed of scientists that are not in your research field (I have participated as a reviewer in such panels). 

Some things that I remind myself of when communicating my research to multi-disciplinary panels are as follows:

- State clearly and immediately the societal (or big picture) relevance of your research area (do not start with technical details). You should write this assuming that the reviewer has little technical background about what you do (which is probably a good guess). Imagine that you are explaining your research to an average person on the street. 

- Be as tutorial as possible and *educate* the reviewer by providing adequate background information. If the reviewer does not have sufficient background about your research area and understands how your specific proposal fits in such area, it will be difficult for them to appreciate relevance and novelty. 

- Remember that, nowadays, every branch of science is really mature/evolved. It is common to assume that everyone understands our jargon and our way of thinking but, trust me, there are *very few* people in the world that understand the technical details of what you do. You are such an specialist that even your advisor (sometimes) does not fully understand the details of what you do :) 

- Provide interesting numbers, anecdotes, or references to pop culture/science that help you connect with the non-expert reviewer. These references can catch the attention of the reviewer. 

- Sometimes you are not only selling your specific research ideas, but you are also selling your research *field*. Multi-disciplinary panels often have to compare proposals across a broad range of disciplines (e.g., materials vs. bioengineering). As such, try to educate the panel on the relevance of your field first and then connect to your specific research area.
