# Embrace Your Naïveté

> *More advice (not part of the series).* Posted on LinkedIn on [November 17, 2023](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7131279902368034817/).

Why is naïveté important in science? 

Being naive often has a negative connotation, as we associate it with being ignorant and lacking judgement. However, one can also associate being naive with being innocent (I prefer this interpretation). 

Throughout my career, being naive has taken me to uncharted territory and to valuable learning experiences. Yes, some of those experiences have led to some failures and hard lessons, but these have been necessary for learning and growing as a scientist. Being naive has also taken me to rabbit holes that have not led to tangible outcomes, but there was definitely learning in the process (e.g., at least I know where not to look again and how much time to invest on things).

You cannot plan ahead and anticipate everything in life (if you could, there would be no learning in the process); some degree of naïveté is needed to yield learning experiences. Yes, like anything in life, you need to strike a balance and use proper judgement/planning , but it is also important to take risks (intentionally and unintentionally). 

Being naive is at the core of science; we often become fascinated by things by random chance (not by calculated moves). So, from time to time celebrate and embrace your naïveté, it is part of the fun in science. We should not be so hard on ourselves when making mistakes, it is part of the learning process.
