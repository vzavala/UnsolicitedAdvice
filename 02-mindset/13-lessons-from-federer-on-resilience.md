# Lessons from Federer on Resilience

> Posted on LinkedIn on [June 14, 2025](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7339645221036617729/).

I recently came across a commencement speech of Roger Federer in which he talked about mental focus/resilience.

He mentioned a shocking statistic: throughout his professional career, he won 80% of his games, but he only won 54% of the points. 

There are a lot of interesting insights that come from this difference. He mentions that this illustrates how you cannot get distracted by short-term loses; if you lose a point, you learn and move on... you need to stay present/focused on the next point you are playing. It is also important to stay particularly focused on the points that truly matter.

His perspective really resonated with me based on my experiences as a scientist. You try many things and many fail; you learn from the failure and move on (do not linger). You try to stay focused/present on the next experiment/trial. Same with papers/proposals rejected and with bad lectures/presentations you give. You try your best and if they don't work you analyze why they didn't work and move on.

I think his perspective is a great reminder of how to build resilience and how to deal with failure. It is very easy to internalize and get distracted by short-term failures, while we are in fact growing and making gains in the long run. To be resilient, I feel that it is important not to lose perspective of long-term gains/goals (e.g., Why are we doing what we are doing? What is my final goal?)
