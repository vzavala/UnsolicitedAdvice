# Be Careful When You Compare Yourself to Others

> *Unsolicited advice for young scientists.* Posted on LinkedIn on [May 2, 2024](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7191781099356721153/).

Be careful when you compare yourself to others. When I started my PhD, I thought that I would never reach the level of senior PhDs/postdocs and I also didn't quite understand if my skills, work, and perspectives were gonna be valuable. Same happened in my professional career; when I got started, it was difficult to appreciate if I was "good enough." 

Running a marathon some years ago gave me some useful perspectives on this subject. When you are running/training, it is natural to compare your times, your pace, how many marathons you have run, if you have run ultramarathons, if you have run barefoot, etc (the number of metrics for comparisons is endless). 

Some things that I learned are the following:

- There was always going to be someone faster and better than me; it was unfair for me to compare metrics (e.g., time). At some point I realized that comparing myself to others was useful to gain perspective but was also detrimental to my own progress. I eventually decided that I needed to focus on monitoring my progress. Same in science, focus on your progress; yes, it is good to see what others are doing to put things in perspective but also don't lose sight of your own improvement. 

- People have different goals when training for a marathon; e.g., some people do it as a hobby and some are professionals. I did it because I wanted to prove to myself that I could do it. Even if you are the slowest runner in the race, completing a marathon is already a giant accomplishment. Getting a PhD is a giant accomplishment and it doesn't matter how you get there. When you compare yourself to others, remember that people have different goals, backgrounds, obstacles, and drivers in mind. 

- Don't forget about the race. When you are comparing yourself, you are losing sight of the most important aspect: enjoying the race. Doing science is fun and you should enjoy it; obsessing over metrics and comparisons can take away the fun. 

- Pace yourself and know your limits. Comparisons can lead to unhealthy behavior. I remember that, at some point, I was pushing myself too hard to increase distance/pace and I got myself injured. I was simply not ready. Same in science, it is great to push forward but learn to understand your limits. Working too hard can lead to unhealthy behavior.
