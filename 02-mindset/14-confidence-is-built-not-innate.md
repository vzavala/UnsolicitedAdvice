# Confidence Is Built, Not Innate

> *More advice (not part of the series).* Posted on LinkedIn on [April 20, 2025](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7319704543296962560/).

I recently came across an interview of former President Barack Obama in which he explained that, a key factor behind success, is *confidence.*

However, he also observed that confidence is not innate, this is something that is gained progressively over time and that is a strong function of your environment (e.g., your upbringing and the circles/networks in which you operate).

This strongly resonated with my experiences with mentorship.

For example, I have no one in my family with a PhD and, as a result, it was difficult for me to assess how well I was doing in grad school and it took me quite some time to build confidence. This is a common feeling among first-gen students or students that do not have proper support networks. In my case, feedback/support from my PhD advisor and from other professors in my community was critical for me to build confidence. 

Also, being in an inclusive environment strongly affects the ability of people to build confidence. For example, if you are in a group that does not respect/listen to your ideas, you are less likely to build confidence. 

I feel that a key goal of effective mentorship is to help our mentees build up confidence. Be open and listen to their ideas, encourage them to take risks, give frequent feedback, and help them navigate failure.
