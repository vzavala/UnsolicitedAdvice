# Understand the Scientific Enterprise

> *Unsolicited advice for young scientists.* Posted on LinkedIn on [November 27, 2022](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7002695356387917824/).

It is important to understand how the scientific enterprise works (e.g., who is funding my research? how does my research benefit my sponsors/society/taxpayers? how do my results can benefit education and advance knowledge? how can I get involved in outreach and dissemination? how does my science can influence policy/law?) 

I feel that having this broad understanding can help develop a sense of purpose and to remain motivated. Sometimes it is easy to get absorbed into the technical details of our own research and we can lose track of why we are doing this and who is this benefiting. 

It is also important to remind ourselves that the scientific enterprise is a circular value chain that involves several components such as fundamental research, applied research, technology transfer (e.g., scale-up, patent law, start-ups), education/training, scientific communication, administration (e.g., university, program managers). For instance, education and training of new scientists circles back to generate more research and perpetuates the science machine. 

I think that it is important to be aware of all of these dimensions because different people contribute in different and valuable ways to make science impactful and because we should understand how we "fit in"; in addition, this can help us understand the many ways in which one can develop a career in science.
