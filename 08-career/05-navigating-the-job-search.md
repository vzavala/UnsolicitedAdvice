# Navigating the Job Search

> *Unsolicited advice for young scientists.* Posted on LinkedIn on [December 1, 2024](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7268996351479209985/).

Job applications/interviews can trigger significant excitement and anxiety; here are some things I wish I could go back in time to remind myself of when I was looking for a job.

- Try to enjoy the interview process and try to stay focused/present; there are *few* times in your life when you will feel this level of excitement and anxiety. All these intense feelings will help you grow professionally and personally; you will develop mechanisms to cope with them. I still remember the anxiety that I was feeling when giving my first interview seminar or talking to a famous scientist in my area (I can almost feel it when I think of this). I wish I could go back in time and relive those moments; in retrospect, that anxiety was triggered by excitement (although I did not recognize it as that at the moment). I also learned to control my anxiety in future interviews and learned to stay more present. 

- The interview process will teach you a lot about yourself; you will be doing a lot of reflection about your career/life goals, skills, and areas of improvement. I still remember a senior scientist asking me during an interview: "What do you want to be known for?" Appears like a simple question, but I did not have a clear answer at the time and this led to a lot of soul searching and reflection that helped me reassess my career goals.

- The interview process will help you meet so many awesome people in diverse areas, it is a great networking opportunity. Those networks/connections might become helpful in the future, regardless of the outcome of your search. 

- Negative outcomes in your search can feel devastating, be prepared to deal with such feelings. I have been rejected many times throughout my career... not fun. In retrospect, I wish I had a better system in place to remind myself of my worth in those difficult times and to remind myself that rejections are often the result of factors that are not under my control. Also, negative outcomes will trigger learning and growth!
