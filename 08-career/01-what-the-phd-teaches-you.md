# What the PhD Teaches You

> *Unsolicited advice for young scientists.* Posted on LinkedIn on [April 18, 2025](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7318974173219811328/).

The PhD is a comprehensive training experience from a professional and personal standpoint. 

By being invested in a specific set of problems/questions over a sustained period of time, you become a true expert in diverse subjects.

However, as you start approaching the boundaries of knowledge (entering uncharted territory) you will face significant uncertainty. This makes you capable of dealing with ambiguity/risk and gives you strategic planning skills (e.g., Where should we go next? Is it worth it?)

You are bombarded with diverse tasks (e.g., experiments, writing papers, meetings, coding, courses, seminars, conferences) that evolve at different timescales. This helps you develop strong time management skills. 

Research involves frequent failure, as many things you try will not work (and some of your work might be rejected). This is an important learning experience that gives you resilience, persistence, and promotes creative thinking. 

You will interact with so many different types of stakeholders (e.g., Professors, other PhDs, collaborators in industry, undergrad students). This gives you strong collaborative and networking skills.

You will frequently have to write papers and give presentations/lectures. This gives you strong communication skills. 

From a personal stand-point… as you face all these challenges, you will learn a lot about yourself (e.g., what are your priorities/goals, what are your unique skills, and how you respond to failure and stress).
