# Sketch Your Talk Before Making Slides

> *Unsolicited advice for young scientists.* Posted on LinkedIn on [July 7, 2024](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7215748873699438592/).

When preparing a talk, it is helpful to create a sketch of the outline, key messages, and content that you want to include. This should be done before preparing/designing any slides.

This sketch will help you organize your thoughts and gain a high-level perspective of the talk that helps you build a coherent narrative/messaging (see how everything connects).

This sketch can also help you mitigate anxiety/uncertainty (which often originates from lack of planning/preparation). It is also much easier to experiment/correct the sketch than to experiment/correct as you are putting together your slides. 

Putting together a talk semi-randomly (without planning) can lead to several issues: lack of coherency/continuity, weak/redundant messaging, lots of corrections/iterations, and stress.
