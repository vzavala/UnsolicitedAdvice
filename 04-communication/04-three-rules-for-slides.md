# Three Rules for Slides

> *Unsolicited advice for young scientists.* Posted on LinkedIn on [February 15, 2025](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7296344249174736896/).

This week I have been chatting with some of my students about how to give presentations. This is all an art but, over the years, I continue to converge to the following 3 points that define my own style:

- Make slides as *simple* as possible. I do appreciate a nice template with logos of your institution(s) in every slide and a nice color scheme, but some of these templates add unnecessary visual distraction. Focus the audience on the main content and try to avoid any unnecessary information (text or visual). Prioritize visual content that is engaging, try to provide concise sentences (1-2 per slide), and minimize words as much as possible. If you have a busy slide, split the content over multiple slides.

- Make slides self-explanatory. The content of the slides should be sufficient for people to follow the take-home messages (without having the speaker to narrate them). In other words, if I share the slides with someone via e-mail, they should be able to follow the narrative of the talk without me being there to assist them. This means that your slides should have enough content for people to grasp the main ideas. 

- The speaker should complement the slides. The slides already tell the story by themselves (if you design them correctly), so… What is your role as a speaker? You should not regurgitate the content of the slides, this would introduce redundant information. Your role as a speaker is to guide the overall narrative and convey emotion/excitement (something that slides cannot do themselves). You, as the speaker, are also there to share stories/anecdotes/jokes that "hook" the audience and keep it engaged. 

There are no fixed rules here. I have broken my own rules many times depending on context or if I feel like experimenting with something new. Giving talks is a learning process and you should definitely try to identify your own style.
