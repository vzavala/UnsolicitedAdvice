# Get to the Point: Lead with Results

> *Unsolicited advice for young scientists.* Posted on LinkedIn on [October 4, 2023](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7115363699019698176/).

In academia we love to use a narrative structure when giving presentations (e.g., motivation->work->conclusions). We love building up "suspense" prior to revealing our results in a big bang :) 

I think this structure is appropriate in some settings but not in others; one thing that I learned when working in industry is that some presentations need to get to the point fast because people have limited time (e.g., don't tell me why, tell me how). For instance, someone in industry once told me that I should start the presentation with the results (or a quick summary of the main results), as this is what people will remember; you do not want to "bury" your results in your narrative. 

Overall, I think that it is important to be mindful that people have surprisingly short attention spans and that people get tired quickly; as such, we should develop strategies to emphasize the key takeaway points often (e.g., tell them what you are gonna tell them, tell them, and tell them what you just told them).
