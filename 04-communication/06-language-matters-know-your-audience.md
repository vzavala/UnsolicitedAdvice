# Language Matters: Know Your Audience

> *Unsolicited advice for young scientists.* Posted on LinkedIn on [March 1, 2024](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7169350701586866177/).

When I was in my postdoc, I remember a senior scientist telling me... "try not to use the word *control* if you present your work to a congressperson, maybe use *optimization*... the word *control* has political connotations." At the time I found this to be amusing but, when I eventually had the chance to present my work to a politician, I realized that language does matter, and it matters *a lot*. 

One needs to be sensitive and aware of the background of the audience, their level of expertise, and their interests. One should also be super careful about jargon and one should try to find simple terms to explain our research. How would you explain your work to a kid without using any jargon? This is actually super challenging, but it is helpful to do this exercise from time to time; this will help you understand your work at a deeper level and it will help you develop the ability to speak "multiple scientific languages" (you will become a "science polyglot"). This will eventually help you strengthen your scientific communication skills and have broader impact.
