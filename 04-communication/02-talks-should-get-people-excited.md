# Talks Should Get People Excited

> *Unsolicited advice for young scientists.* Posted on LinkedIn on [October 9, 2025](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7382153851707215872/).

In my opinion, your primary goal when giving a presentation is to get people excited.

My colleague Michael Graham recently used the term "infectious enthusiasm." I like this term a lot, as it perfectly describes how the audience feels after an amazing talk.

Getting people excited requires that you are excited when giving a talk. Talks that are monotone in delivery do not get people excited; talks that do not talk about why the results are relevant do not get people excited; talks that do not carefully articulate ideas do not get people excited. 

Excitement is what leads people to want to learn more about what you do and about the topic. Excitement is also memorable; I still remember the talks or lectures that got me excited 20+ yrs ago. You want that your talk creates a lasting impression on people.
