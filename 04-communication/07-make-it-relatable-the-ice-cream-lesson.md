# Make It Relatable: The Ice-Cream Lesson

> *Unsolicited advice for young scientists.* Posted on LinkedIn on [May 9, 2025](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7326395806909898752/).

A few years ago I was chatting with a PhD student about how to communicate our research on the environmental impacts of dairy waste to broad audiences. This conversation was triggered because it seemed to me that, in my talks, people were not particularly excited to learn about cow waste, I don't blame them :) 

We decided to run an experiment in which we were going to communicate the impact of the dairy industry in terms of ice cream. Why? Because we hypothesized that people have more emotional attachment to ice cream than to milk or waste.

The results were striking; when we started linking economic and environmental impacts of the dairy industry to ice cream, audiences were more receptive and engaged. People started coming after my talks telling me how surprised they were to learn that a single cow could produce so much ice cream (in milk equivalents) or of how much waste is produced in the dairy supply chain per gallon of ice cream produced.

I think it is super important to think about the emotional impact of the metrics/narratives we use when communicating the relevance of our research to broad audiences. People do care about science but do not tend to think in technical/abstract terms; they need to be able to relate how our work connects to something they deeply care about. This is not easy to do by ourselves; as such, I feel that it is super important to interact more with social scientists and experts in scientific communication.
