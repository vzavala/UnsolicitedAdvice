# Networking, Especially for Introverts

> *Unsolicited advice for young scientists.* Posted on LinkedIn on [April 7, 2024](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7182872626225164288/).

Networking is one of the most important aspects of developing a scientific career. This is because it is important that people (academics and industry) are aware of your work for you to have impact. Moreover, networking is an essential part of identifying research collaborations and of finding jobs and funding.

However, when I was a student, I had no clear idea on how I could begin "building my networks". Also, as an introvert, in-person networking (e.g., at conferences) did not come naturally to me. 

There are several mechanisms that I have used/discovered throughout my career to do networking:

- Talks/posters: Presenting your work is one of the most traditional and effective ways to communicate your research and to "build a presence" in the community. Try to find opportunities to present your work at your institution and at conferences. You should also find opportunities to give lectures and participate in outreach events. 

- Talk to people at conferences: Conferences exist to build networks and they provide an important opportunity for you to immerse yourself in the scientific community. A natural way to talk to people at conferences is by approaching them after their talks. If you feel shy/intimidated, it is easier to have an "excuse" to go and talk to people (e.g., asking people questions after their talks). People will also naturally come and talk to you after your presentations. 

- Volunteer: Volunteering to participate in organizations is an effective way to meet people. For instance, conferences often include networking events for students. Try to participate in these, it is easier to meet people at events that have been designed for this specific purpose. 

- Social media: As an introvert, social media has been a game changer, because it gives me a "safer" and more comfortable space to communicate my work and to meet people in an informal manner. I also find it easier to communicate my ideas in writing. Social media also offers many tools to build a profile (e.g., develop tutorials). 

- Ask for introductions: Mentors play a critical role in getting you integrated in the scientific community. Ask your mentors (or your existing connections) to introduce you to people or to tell you which people you should meet. 

You should also realize that your network tends to grow fast as you become more experienced. In addition, you become more comfortable establishing connections (with practice you become less shy). Be patient, I still remember the early days of my career in which I knew literally noone at conferences and now I feel like I know everyone. I enjoy attending conferences and having the opportunity to catch up with the people that I know; I see many members of my network so regularly that they have become "family."
