# About the Series

The book includes posts from **November 27, 2022** to **October 27, 2026**: 134 posts over almost four years. 87 of them open with "Unsolicited advice…"; the other 47 give the same kind of advice without that opener. Nearly every post carries the hashtag #phdresearch or #academia.

## How the series started

The first post, on November 27, 2022, was about understanding how the scientific enterprise works: who funds research, who benefits from it, and where each of us fits. More posts followed, and by mid-2023 the series had a steady rhythm.

The idea behind every post is simple: to write down the things I wish someone had told me when I was younger, particularly when I was struggling. Many of them seem basic and are easy to take for granted, but they can make a big difference when you're starting a career in science.

The series grew because readers kept asking for more. At the AIChE Annual Meeting in 2024 and at ESCAPE-35 in 2025, students, postdocs and professors stopped to say they appreciated the "unsolicited advice" and encouraged me to keep writing. One reader wrote that every post had been "either a great wake-up call, a good nudge, or a much needed reminder."

Those conversations also shaped the purpose of the series. Scientific and academic environments tend to promote doubt, uncertainty, stress and anxiety, and they shouldn't. Open conversations, breaking taboos and more emotional intelligence can help with many of these problems. Several posts here, on anxiety, depression, impostor syndrome and criticism, are part of that effort.

In October 2026 the posts were collected into this book and organized by theme instead of by date.
