# Treat Your Research Products as Investments

> *Unsolicited advice for young scientists.* Posted on LinkedIn on [July 26, 2023](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7090113973773062144/).

Think about your research products (e.g., data, models, code, experiments, figures, papers, slides, proposals) as an investment. 

One thing that I have realized over my career is that I end up re-using products over and over again (e.g., I still use figures, slides, code that I put together when I was in my PhD ~20 yrs ago). 

As such, some of the things that I try to remind myself of are the following:

- If you are going to put together figures and slides, do the best job possible. Some of those figures and slides will be used in your thesis document, thesis defense, and in multiple presentations (including job interviews). You do not want to be revisiting/re-doing figures and slides many times, as this consumes a lot time. 

- The first time you produce something (particularly a paper or a proposal), it feels like a giant endeavor that takes a lot of time. However, once you figure out the framework/strategy for doing this, things get *much* easier. As such, think about writing a paper/proposal as an investment from which you will learn a strategy (how to play the game). 

- Try to minimize waste. Try to generate data, models, code, figures, documents that will likely generate value in the future; otherwise you are just wasting your time/energy in something that might be a "one-time gig." Of course, research is unpredictable and one might not see the value right away. 

I think that it is important to think about products as an investment, as this helps you strategize on how to best allocate your time/energy and prioritize. Moreover, I feel that knowing that your products will be used in the future can help you stay motivated.
