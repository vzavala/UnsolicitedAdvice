# Take Good Meeting Notes

> *Unsolicited advice for young scientists.* Posted on LinkedIn on [October 17, 2024](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7252723428581879808/).

Learn to document technical discussions by taking *good* meeting notes. 

When I was younger, I thought that I could remember all aspects of technical discussions with my advisor/collaborators. However, I quickly found out that I would forget technical details easily and sometimes I would forget to follow through with some key ideas/directions. Some people have great memory, but there are limits. 

Research (particularly multidisciplinary) is complex. In a meeting you are brainstorming and bouncing ideas around and often there are no clear outcomes/salient points from the conversation; taking notes can help you discover those salient points. Also, ideas will tend to mature slowly over time (over multiple technical meetings) and notes will help you keep track of them and see how they evolve. 

Through time I have realized that it is super important to properly document discussions. I have moved from drawing scribbles in a notepad to take detailed meeting notes; at times this feels like a lot of work but I have found that taking notes helps me digest/summarize the discussion, helps me structure my thoughts, and helps me structure the directions of a project. Also, ideas accumulate over time and thus keeping track of them helps you prioritize them.
