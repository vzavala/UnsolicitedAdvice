# Build a Research Program Around a Core

> *Unsolicited advice for young faculty.* Posted on LinkedIn on [July 1, 2025](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7345787713700143104/).

Yesterday I was helping one of my trainees frame her future research program. 

One thing that I have learned working at the U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) labs is how to build a research program that meets long-term and short-term research goals. 

The way I think about this is that you need to define a core set of research directions that are based on fundamental research (your core capabilities). Adhering to this core is essential to build strong foundations that are transferable; this gives you flexibility to quickly "pivot/adjust" in response to short-term goals (e.g., applications or new research trends).  I have seen this model working time and time again at the national labs for many years. 

Without having a core mission, one can get easily distracted and/or quickly jump into different applications as a response to funding opportunities (like chasing a chicken). Chasing funding is of course important, but I think one should not forget about maintaining the core elements/principles of your program, as this is the essence that defines you.
