# "Unsolicited Advice" — Key Takeaways for PhD Students and Faculty

A summary of Victor M. Zavala's 82 LinkedIn posts in the series (Nov 2022 – Sep 2026). The source text is in [unsolicited-advice-posts.md](unsolicited-advice-posts.md). Dates in parentheses point to the original posts.

**Who the posts are for:** 61 are addressed to young scientists, 11 to young scientists doing computational or mathematical work, and 10 to young faculty or professors.

---

## The big ideas (the short version)

1. **Your brain is your main tool, so protect it.** Rest makes you more productive. Pace yourself, because a PhD is a marathon. Take mental health seriously and ask for help early.
2. **Asking the right question matters more than answering one.** Make time for focused thinking and for play. Aim for work that opens new problems, and focus on the fundamentals that carry over to new problems.
3. **Communication is part of the science.** Science means generating ideas *and selling them*. Writing, talks, proposals and notation are all ways of thinking clearly as well as sharing results.
4. **The process matters more than the outcome.** Failed papers, rejected proposals, interviews that didn't work out and pushback on your ideas still teach you things, bring collaborators and give you ideas.
5. **People are what make you effective.** Build networks and several mentors. Practice self-awareness and humility, and avoid comparing yourself unfairly with others.
6. **Structure supports creativity.** Plan ahead, set milestones, write down ideas and treat what you produce as an investment. Leave room for free exploration too.
7. **Reflect on purpose and progress regularly.** Know why you do the work and who it helps ("What do you want to be known for?"), and stop to celebrate what you've done.

## For PhD students, postdocs and young scientists

### 1. Well-being and mental health
- Treat your brain like the rest of your body. Rest it, strengthen it, and catch "injuries" early (2023-10-11, 2026-09-23).
- Don't work tired. Set a clear goal for the day, and stop once you've met it (2026-04-19).
- A PhD is a marathon, not a sprint. Sleep well, eat well, exercise, keep up your hobbies and stay in touch with friends and family. Fear and anxiety alongside the excitement are normal, so talk about them openly (2024-09-03).
- **Depression is common in science and can go unnoticed for years.** Victor describes his own experience. Watch for it, seek help, and talk about it openly without judgement. With therapy and reflection, change is possible (2025-05-26).
- Watch out for the "I need to" mindset, which drains motivation. Set aside **play time** to enjoy science for its own sake, without deadlines (2025-11-18).

### 2. Comparison, confidence and resilience
- Be careful about comparing yourself with others. Lessons from marathon running: someone will always be faster, so track your own progress. People have different goals. Enjoy the race. Know your limits (2024-05-02).
- Competitive environments breed insecurity. Help build inclusive cultures that celebrate everyone's contributions, and look inward at what excites you (2025-08-19).
- Good ideas often meet resistance and skepticism. That can mean you've "touched a nerve." Be patient, keep refining your ideas, and teach others how to use them (2024-08-22).
- Question everything and everyone, including textbooks and established ideas. Welcome challenges to your own ideas too, because that's how they get stronger (2026-01-02).

### 3. Choosing and framing research problems
- Finding the right problem is harder than solving one. Ask "What is the question?" and "Is it worth answering?" (2023-02-21).
- Write papers that create problems: ask big questions and open new ways of thinking (2024-06-10).
- Separate the **fundamental** parts of a topic from the **applied** ones, and master the fundamentals. Applications go in and out of fashion, but principles last (2023-08-24).
- Start from a specific application, then ask what fundamental knowledge working on it could produce (2025-08-03).
- **Reality check:** imagine giving a seminar on the direction you're considering. Would the story be exciting? This helps you get out of rabbit holes (2025-02-22).
- Make time for focused thinking (2025-12-26), and practice reductionism: break problems into simpler parts (2025-11-13).
- Look for inspiration in simple places such as nature, sports and daily life (2023-08-11). Look for collaborations with fields that seem unrelated to yours (2023-09-28).
- **Manage your ideas.** Write them down, let them mature, and don't feel you have to pursue them all at once. One idea Victor is working on now came from his postdoc 15 years ago (2023-10-28).
- Have a vision. "What do you want to be known for?" Use it to re-center and to decide what expertise to build (2026-01-07).

### 4. Communication: talks, writing and "selling" ideas
- **Science means selling ideas.** A great idea has to be communicated well to spread. Learn from storytelling, visualization, cognitive psychology, marketing and even comedy (2023-12-12).
- **Talks:** aim for "infectious enthusiasm" (2025-10-09). Sketch the outline and key messages before you design any slides (2024-07-07). Keep slides simple and self-explanatory, and use your voice to carry the story (2025-02-15). Use math sparingly (2024-08-14).
- **Know your audience:** language matters (2024-03-01). For industry audiences or anyone short on time, lead with the results: "tell them what you're going to tell them, tell them, then tell them what you told them" (2023-10-04).
- **Make it relatable:** link your metrics to things people care about emotionally. Dairy-waste impacts expressed in *ice cream* engaged audiences far more (2025-05-09).
- **Writing is thinking:** it helps you organize and remember your own ideas (2025-12-02).
- **Be succinct:** a 2-page document is harder to write than a 15-page one. Use every word for a reason, break up complex sentences, and zoom in and out to find repetition (2025-03-23).
- **Make writing fun:** treat it as an art or a game, and rotate your focus between story, teaching, aesthetics and impact (2023-07-31).
- **Proposals for panels from many fields:** lead with the big-picture relevance, teach the reviewer, avoid jargon, use catchy numbers and analogies, and sell your *field* before your idea (2024-01-17).
- Keep notation consistent and compact (2025-09-05). Speak openly about agreement, disagreement, struggles and failures (2026-05-15).

### 5. Writing papers
- Break a paper into "small wins" and celebrate each one (2025-06-05). Write a skeleton first, then refine it section by section, like a multigrid method (2023-12-22).
- The first paper is the hardest, and the next ones get much easier (2025-04-04). Not every paper has to be a home run (2025-04-29). Being correct matters more than being first (2025-07-30).

### 6. Productivity and organization
- Scientists aren't really absent-minded geniuses. **Time management is an underrated skill.** Structure keeps you productive and helps your work-life balance (2024-08-29).
- Set milestones with real deadlines, using commitment devices like group-meeting presentations (2023-11-12). Take good meeting notes (2024-10-17).
- **Treat what you produce as an investment:** figures, slides, code and data get reused for decades. Do them well the first time and avoid one-off work (2023-07-26).
- Be patient: projects start slowly and then can grow quickly (2024-07-01).

### 7. People, mentors and networks
- **Build a network of trusted mentors.** Your advisor can't be your only one. Include senior students, postdocs, other faculty, industry contacts and staff (2026-03-10).
- **Networking, especially for introverts:** give talks and posters, ask questions after other people's talks, volunteer, use social media, and ask mentors to introduce you. Your network grows quickly with time (2024-04-07).
- Ask colleagues about their career paths. They are often nonlinear and shaped by chance, and the stories inspire (2024-06-23).
- Self-awareness and emotional intelligence (2026-06-28), leadership and collaboration skills (2025-03-02), being a good mentee (2024-09-15), humility (2024-12-30), and time with people from other cultures (2025-05-13).

### 8. Career development
- **Teaching:** being a TA or teaching builds communication skills and deepens your grasp of the fundamentals (2024-09-29, 2025-12-17).
- **Background:** curiosity counts for more than prior experience (2025-03-15). The PhD builds a wide range of skills (2025-04-18).
- **The postdoc:** use it to reflect, explore boldly and build skills beyond research (2024-11-10).
- **Job search:** enjoy it, reflect, network, and prepare for rejection (2024-12-01).
- **Paths and purpose:** there are many career paths (2024-01-25). Understand the scientific enterprise and where you fit (2022-11-27). Use science to navigate life, for example by treating reading the literature as an explore/exploit problem (2023-01-05).

### 9. For computational and mathematical researchers
- **Think before you hit "solve."** In the punched-card era, every run was a carefully designed experiment. Plan and anticipate the outcomes (2025-02-02).
- **Do calculations by hand first** on simplified versions of the problem. You'll build intuition, anticipate results and be able to explain them. That's how his advisor seemed like a "mathemagician" (2024-02-24).
- Build models step by step and validate each step (2023-09-21). **Test your code at the extremes.** Breaking it is how you make it robust (2024-04-16).
- Algorithms don't understand physics, so validate solutions (2024-05-15). Models are useful beyond prediction (2024-01-05).
- **Learn several programming languages.** Comparing them deepens your grasp of your main one and builds abstraction skills (2024-05-29).
- You don't have to be "amazing" at coding or math (2023-10-18). Algorithms can also be ways of thinking about everyday life (2023-12-22).

## For young faculty

### Mentoring
- **Mentoring as feedback control:** early in the PhD, set clear targets and monitor closely, because the goal is building skills, not risky exploration. After 2–3 years, shift to giving "operating regions" with more freedom, while watching for "overshoot." By the end, students should be setting their own course (2023-11-26).
- Trust your students and treat them as professionals (2026-07-06). Give hard feedback with care (2026-03-23).
- Choose research problems for what they'll teach your trainees, not only for the science (2024-07-31).

### Protecting your research time and your joy in it
- Block research time and guard it "religiously" (2023-12-28). Keep doing hands-on research (2024-12-09). Read for pleasure (2025-07-11).
- Reflect on what you've accomplished and celebrate it regularly (2023-08-17).

### Building a research program and community
- Explore broadly early on, then go deeper (2025-03-10). Find problems by listening to senior scientists, your community, society, industry, government, collaborators and your own gut (2024-01-12).
- **Organize events** such as workshops, short courses and conferences. They connect you to the community, spark collaborations, raise your students' visibility and spread knowledge. They take a lot of time, but it pays off (2025-08-27).
- Proposals: focus on the process of developing ideas, not on the money (2022-12-06). Give teams time and the right environment (2024-07-01).

## A quick self-check

**PhD students and postdocs:**
- [ ] Did I rest enough this week, and do I know the warning signs of burnout or depression?
- [ ] Am I tracking my own progress instead of comparing myself with others?
- [ ] Have I spent time on focused thinking, and some time just playing with ideas?
- [ ] Are my ideas written down and prioritized?
- [ ] Do I have several mentors and a growing network?
- [ ] Do I have clear milestones, and am I making things (figures, code, slides) I'll be able to reuse?
- [ ] Could I explain my work without jargon, in a way people can relate to?
- [ ] For a model or algorithm: did I work on a simple version by hand, test it at the extremes, and check the results make physical sense?

**Faculty:**
- [ ] Is my research time blocked off and protected?
- [ ] Is the level of freedom I give each student right for their stage?
- [ ] Am I trusting my students and giving honest feedback with care?
- [ ] Have I organized something recently that brings the community together?
- [ ] Have I looked back recently and celebrated what the group has achieved?
