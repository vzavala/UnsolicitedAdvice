# Impact Doesn't Have to Mean Commercialization

> *Unsolicited advice for young scientists doing computational research.* Posted on LinkedIn on [October 17, 2025](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7384937752498397184/).

This week I was asked about how do I translate my research into commercial products. 

As a computational researcher, I often do not directly commercialize the models, theory/ideas, and software that we develop. In fact, I am a strong believer in open science, but in some occasions our work has helped support patents and is used by companies. 

Computational researchers often provide data/insight/models that are used as evidence to support patents or to support high-impact publications (via collaborative efforts). 

I mention this because I feel that there is a misconception that all research needs to *directly* translate into commercial products for it to be useful. Science is more complicated than that; successful products (think of your  iphone or your Tesla) embed hundreds of patents (e.g., sensors, processors, batteries, materials, math algorithms) and fundamental knowledge that made these possible. 

Science is a community that generates, exchanges, and refines ideas; we support one another and sometimes we do not expect anything in return... we often just want to help solve problems and ultimately help society. 

Needless to say, there are computational researchers that are incredibly successful in commercializing their work; I admire that ability tremendously!
