# You Don't Have to Be Amazing at Coding or Math

> *Unsolicited advice for young scientists doing computational research.* Posted on LinkedIn on [October 18, 2023](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7120213249228247040/).

There is a perception that you have to be "amazing" at programming & math to make an impact in computational research. This perception can lead to feelings of inadequacy and to impostor syndrome.

There is a broad spectrum of levels (and types) of proficiency in coding/math used in research. You can make an impact by using your coding/math skills to implement models, you can make an impact by using your skills for identifying new applications, you can make an impact by using your skills for teaching, you can make an impact by developing software packages, you can make an impact by doing theory, you can make an impact by doing outreach, you can make an impact by analyzing experimental data... 

I personally have never felt that I am particularly good at (or particularly excited about) coding... I use programming as a means to an end (e.g., solve practical problems or testing algorithms that I develop). Also, I personally have never felt to be particularly good at math (everyone probably feels the same way!)... but I truly enjoy identifying engineering applications of math. 

Ultimately, I feel that finding what you are truly passionate about is what matters; develop and use your coding/math skills to pursue such passions.
