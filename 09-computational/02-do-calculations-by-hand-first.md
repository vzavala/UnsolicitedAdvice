# Do Calculations by Hand First

> *Unsolicited advice for young scientists doing computational modeling.* Posted on LinkedIn on [February 24, 2024](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7167163381718732800/).

There is something beautiful/powerful about doing calculations/analysis/derivations *by hand*. Before relying on the computer to do this for you on the full problem, it is extremely helpful to first analyze a simplified version of the problem by hand. 

Doing calculations/derivations by hand will help you gain insight into the problem and this, in turn, can help you anticipate the results that you will obtain when solving the full model (e.g., it will help you frame hypotheses). This preliminary insight will also help you analyze/explain the solutions that you obtain with your computational model. 

My biggest concern is that, once you solve the full problem on a computer, it might be difficult to explain the solution or to figure out why the computer is suggesting such a solution. Doing some preliminary analysis yourself can help you diagnose this. 

Doing things by hand will also force you to *think* and it will help you strengthen your math skills. I love reading "old" books in which the authors needed to figure out creative math approaches to deal with their problems (because they had no easy access to computers). Yes, one may argue that the problems they could solve back then were not as sophisticated/complex, but such scientists had deep insight into their problems. 

As you become more experienced, doing derivations by hand will help you develop an instinct on how you expect things to pan out. I always admired the ability of my PhD advisor to anticipate the solution of an optimization problem (before me solving it). I thought that he was some sort of “mathemagician", but the reality is that he spent a lot of time doing calculations/derivations by hand. 

I think relying less on computers is helpful in this age of artificial intelligence / machine learning; maybe I am getting old, but doing things by hand helps me disconnect, relax, and enjoy the process of doing science. I am finding myself doing more of this lately; I develop such a deep sense of pride in my results when I do not need to rely on the machine :)
