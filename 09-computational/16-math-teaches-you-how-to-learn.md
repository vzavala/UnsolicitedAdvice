# Math Teaches You How to Learn

> *Unsolicited advice for young scientists doing computational research.* Posted on LinkedIn on [January 30, 2025](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7290719210417111040/).

Just listened to an interview of Cleve Moler (creator of Matlab) on the value of learning mathematics. 

He said something that resonated with me... the actual value of the math you learn in school is important but limited (e.g., the problem that you work on in your PhD thesis is most likely not what you will be doing in your industrial career). 

He argues that the true value of math is that it gives you a systematic process to "learn how to learn." 

I think this is a great reminder of the value of education and on how we should approach education. For example, I teach feedback control, and many times I hear the argument that "Laplace transforms are not used in industry, let's stop teaching them." 

It is the process of learning and deriving Laplace transforms what is truly valuable (it fosters a systematic and abstract way of thinking), that mental process is what you take with you after graduation and what is broadly applicable. 

I think this is relevant as we discuss what to teach and how to teach it (particularly in this era of artificial intelligence); it is not as easy as it seems, and this challenge is what I find fascinating about education.
