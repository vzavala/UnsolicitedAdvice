# How to Use This Book

Each entry is short and stands on its own, so you don't need to read the book in order.

**Find what applies to you now.**

- **Just starting a PhD?** Begin with [Part 1: Well-Being](01-well-being/index.md) and [Part 2: Mindset](02-mindset/index.md), especially "It's Normal to Start a PhD Without Experience."
- **In the middle of research?** [Part 3: Choosing Problems](03-problems-and-ideas/index.md), [Part 5: Writing](05-writing/index.md) and [Part 6: Productivity](06-productivity/index.md).
- **Preparing a talk or proposal?** [Part 4: Communicating Science](04-communication/index.md) and the proposal entries in Part 5.
- **Working with models, code or math?** [Part 9: Computational and Mathematical Research](09-computational/index.md).
- **A postdoc or on the job market?** [Part 8: Career Development](08-career/index.md) and [Part 7: Mentors and Networks](07-people/index.md).
- **New faculty?** [Part 10: For Young Faculty](10-faculty/index.md). Parts 1 and 4 are still worth a look.

**Use it in a group.** Many entries work well as a five-minute prompt for discussion at a group meeting, in an orientation session or in a mentoring conversation. Read one, then ask what the group agrees or disagrees with.

**Check in periodically.** The [self-check lists](checklists.md) are meant to be revisited every few months.

Each entry links to the original LinkedIn post, where you can also read the discussion in the comments.
