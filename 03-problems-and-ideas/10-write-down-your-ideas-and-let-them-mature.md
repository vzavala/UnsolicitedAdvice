# Write Down Your Ideas and Let Them Mature

> *Unsolicited advice for young scientists.* Posted on LinkedIn on [October 28, 2023](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7124020760586682368/).

Throughout your career, you will come up with lots of ideas/problems that you can potentially work on. At times this can feel overwhelming/stressful, because you have limited time and you have other priorities that you need to pay attention to (e.g., existing collaborations). At times you might also feel a sense of urgency to pursue these ideas (as other researchers around the world might pursue them first!). 

Document your ideas; write them down in a TO-DO list (do not let them sit in your brain or you might forget them!). Writing down your ideas will help you structure your thoughts (important to reduce anxiety) and will also help you make sense of their urgency/priority. 

Remember that you do not need to execute all your ideas right away; I can tell you that I am currently working on an idea that I had 15 years ago during my postdoc (at the time I did not feel too strongly about that idea but I do now). For an idea to be executed *well* you need adequate time/resources that might become available in the future. Also, some ideas need to "mature" prior to being executed; let your ideas rest for a bit, revisit them from time to time, obtain evidence/prelim results, and discuss them with people (to convince yourself that they are worth pursuing). As you become more experienced, you will become more selective. I can tell you that there are many ideas that, in retrospect, I should have probably not pursued. 

What I like about science is this continuous "game" of generating/discarding/developing ideas and finding the right time to execute them. 

I really love the quote of Victor Hugo... "Nothing is more powerful than an idea whose time has come."
