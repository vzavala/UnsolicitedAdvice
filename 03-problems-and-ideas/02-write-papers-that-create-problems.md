# Write Papers That Create Problems

> *Unsolicited advice for young scientists.* Posted on LinkedIn on [June 10, 2024](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7206082516229791744/).

Someone once told me that it is great to write papers that solve problems but that it is even better to write papers that “create problems.” 

As an engineer, I was trained to solve problems; as such, I initially found this way of thinking to be quite confusing.

But now, as a “scientist,” it does make sense to me. It makes sense because creating problems is correlated with asking questions. 

You want to do research that asks big/deep questions. In a single paper you will not be able to answer the big question (or solve the big problem), but you might motivate others to start working on the same problem and join you in trying to solve it. You want to write papers that open new ways of thinking and new paradigms to looking at problems. 

Also, if we solve a problem in a paper, then… What is left for us or others to do? Is this the end? If so, maybe the problem/question we are addressing was not deep/big enough. 

Of course, not every paper can be a “paradigm shift,” but I think it is a useful exercise for us to think about what are the implications of our results, what else is left to do (what are the open questions), how do our results generalize, and so on.

So, create big problems and ask big questions… I guess this why we scientists have always been seen as “trouble-makers” :)
