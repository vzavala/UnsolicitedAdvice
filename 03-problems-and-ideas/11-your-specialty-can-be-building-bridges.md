# Your Specialty Can Be Building Bridges

> Posted on LinkedIn on [July 21, 2025](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7353145593147346945/).

One of the really cool aspects of working in systems engineering is that you tend to sit at an intersection of fields/areas. As a result, you get exposed to ideas, concepts, and approaches from many different communities (e.g., math, physics, all engineering, chemistry, economics, social sciences, computer science). 

Being at the intersection of fields helps u spot similarities and differences in the way that different communities think and approach problems. More often than not, I find that there are “universal” approaches that people tend to follow (but under different contexts or nomenclature). I always get excited when I find out that 2 distinct communities have been using the same approach, apparently without talking to each other :) 

As a systems engineer, I often worry that I do not have an area of specialization/expertise, as I sit at the interface of many areas. It has taken me many years to come to the realization that the specialization/expertise of a systems engineer is *precisely* to find/establish bridges across areas. This is not an easy task, as you need to develop the ability to think abstractly and to speak many scientific languages!
