# Start from a Specific Application

> *Unsolicited advice for young scientists.* Posted on LinkedIn on [August 3, 2025](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7357704279874301952/).

A strategy that I use to identify research problems to work on is to think of a *specific* application that will benefit from such research.

For instance, I think of an application that will benefit a company, society, or a collaborator. As I select such application, I also think if there is *fundamental* new knowledge that can be obtained by addressing such application, as this is what will allow me to generalize/transfer results to other applications.

I find this strategy helpful, because there is a vast sea of interesting research problems that one can work on and our time is limited. I also find it overwhelming at times to decide what to work on next and I dont want the decision to be random. I need a strategy to narrow down the choice. 

This is one of many strategies that people use; sometimes I follow intuition and sometimes I start with a fundamental question that might no have immediate application. Selecting a research problem is a creative process and is one of the main skills and exciting aspects of being a scientist.
