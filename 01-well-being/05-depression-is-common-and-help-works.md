# Depression Is Common, and Help Works

> *Unsolicited advice for young scientists.* Posted on LinkedIn on [May 26, 2025](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7332740589060313088/).

When I was younger I faced significant depression. This is more common than not in society and tends to magnify in scientific environments (e.g., due to sustained periods of isolation, strong competition, high expectations). 

When I was in my PhD, I was fortunate enough to realize that I was depressed and I searched for help at the right time. What was a shock for me was that, according to the doctor, depression likely originated for me when I was in my early teens (I had been dealing with it for over 10 years without me noticing). This made me realize that depression is an elusive and persistent issue that can go unnoticed and that can "flare up" at specific times. 

Dealing with depression has been a long journey that has required constant self-reflection and therapy for many years. But, over time, I have realized that change is possible. It is possible to literally "re-wire" your brain to think positively, to be resilient (to bounce back after traumatic events), and to think of challenges/failures as opportunities. This is the way I have trained myself to think and it feels like a huge relief; also, with age comes a lot of perspective/wisdom and you realize that there are many things that we unnecessarily worry about.

There is no unique recipe/approach to deal with depression; the best start is to monitor it, identify it, and seek appropriate help. I also encourage everyone to openly talk about this and avoid judgement, as this facilitates early detection/diagnosis. Also, remember that depression can show up in many shapes and forms and remember that it can happen to anyone.
